# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This documentation describes OctoAcme's practices for coordinating cross-functional projects and delivering customer value through iterative, measurable work.

OctoAcme follows a project lifecycle that begins by validating a business need, aligning stakeholders, and defining measurable success criteria. Once approved, the team plans scope into prioritized backlog items with acceptance criteria, estimates, milestones, a Definition of Done, and identified dependencies. Delivery proceeds in small increments, with progress tracked on a shared project board; releases are verified after deployment, and retrospectives capture lessons as actionable improvements.

Responsibilities are shared across clearly defined roles. Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, resources, and communications; Product Managers set outcomes, prioritize the backlog, and measure impact. Developers build and test the solution, QA validates acceptance, and stakeholders provide input and approvals. Clear ownership for work, risks, and dependencies helps the team resolve blockers and keep decisions visible.

Communication combines team coordination and stakeholder updates: standups surface progress and blockers, weekly delivery or PM syncs review status and risks, and demos or milestone reviews show outcomes. A shared source of truth supports regular updates, while escalation moves from the delivery team through the PM and Product Lead to the sponsor when needed.

Quality is integrated throughout delivery through unit and integration tests, CI checks for tests and linting, code review, and security scanning. Before release, acceptance criteria and CI must pass; teams prepare release notes, rollback or mitigation plans, and smoke tests for staging and post-deployment verification. Retrospectives after sprints, releases, or incidents turn learnings into a small set of owned, trackable improvement actions.
